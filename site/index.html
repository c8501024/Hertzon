Hertzon

A free, open-source 3D full-wave electromagnetic simulator with a classic engineering workflow: draw the model, assign boundaries and ports, solve with adaptive meshing, then look at S-parameters, fields and radiation patterns. The finite-element solver underneath is EMerge (2nd-order Nédélec FEM).

By Dominik Mair, built on EMerge, the FEM solver by Robert Fennis.

What you can simulate

  • Antennas: patch and dipole antennas with far field, gain, directivity, beamwidth and 3D radiation patterns
  • Microwave circuits: microstrip lines and filters with S-parameters, Smith charts and frequency sweeps
  • Waveguides and coaxial lines: wave ports, modes and field plots
  • Radar cross section (RCS) with incident plane waves
  • PCB designs: Gerber export of the simulated layout

Why Hertzon

  • Finite-element method (FEM) frequency-domain solver with adaptive mesh refinement until S-parameters converge
  • Built on EMerge: its adaptive refinement (AURA), error estimate and curved elements, plus Hertzon's own local Delaunay mesher (hzmesh) as an alternative
  • Fast and interpolating frequency sweeps, parallel sweep points, optimization and parametric sweeps
  • Derivative tuning: sliders for design variables with S-parameters and patterns following live, and an optimizer that works on the tuning model
  • Solve on another computer in the network or on a cloud server created for the session (experimental)
  • Half and quarter models with symmetry planes: the far field includes the mirror images
  • Desktop GUI (Qt + VTK) that stays responsive with large models; the solver runs in its own process
  • Python scripting and an MCP server, so AI assistants can build and run simulations
  • Free and open source under the GPL, for Windows and Linux

Get started

Download the installer from the releases page, then follow the step-by-step tutorial: a waveguide with wave ports, a coaxial cable, a half-wave dipole and the RCS of a sphere, each from the empty window to the result. More: feature matrix, benchmarks, changelog.
